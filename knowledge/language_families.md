# Indian Language Families

Indian languages belong to four major language families: Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic, and Sino-Tibetan.

The Indo-Aryan family is the largest, covering approximately 75 percent of India's population. These languages descend from Sanskrit and include Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, Assamese, Sindhi, Urdu, Nepali, Kashmiri, Maithili, Konkani, Dogri, and Sindhi. They are spoken primarily in northern, western, and central India.

The Dravidian family covers approximately 20 percent of India's population. The four major Dravidian languages are Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Malayalam, all spoken in southern India. These languages have no proven connection to Indo-Aryan languages and developed independently. Tamil is the oldest, with a literary tradition going back over 2,000 years.

The Austroasiatic family includes the Munda languages spoken in central and eastern India. Santali is the most widely spoken Munda language with approximately 7 million speakers, and it is one of the 22 scheduled languages in the Indian Constitution.

The Sino-Tibetan family includes languages spoken in northeastern India and the Himalayan regions. Manipuri (Meitei) and Bodo are scheduled languages from this family. Many languages of Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram, and Sikkim also belong to this family.

Sanskrit is the classical language of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism and is the ancestor of all modern Indo-Aryan languages. It remains one of the 22 scheduled languages of India, with approximately 24,000 people reporting it as their mother tongue in the 2001 census. Sanskrit has had an enormous influence on the vocabulary of virtually all Indian languages, including Dravidian ones.

The total number of languages spoken in India is estimated between 122 and over 1,600, depending on whether closely related varieties are counted as dialects or separate languages.
