# Indic Scripts

India uses a large variety of scripts, most of them descended from the ancient Brahmi script. Most Indian scripts are abugidas, meaning they are consonant-based alphabets where vowels are indicated by diacritical marks attached to consonants.

Devanagari is the most widely used Indic script. It is used to write Hindi, Marathi, Sanskrit, Nepali, Konkani, and several other languages. Devanagari has 11 vowels, 33 consonants, and various conjunct consonants formed by combining consonants. The script is written left to right and features a horizontal bar (matra) at the top of letters.

Tamil script is unique among Indic scripts. It uses only 12 vowels and 18 pure consonants, relying on compound characters for other sounds. This gives Tamil script exactly 247 characters. The script has remained remarkably stable for over a thousand years.

Bengali script is used for Bengali and Assamese. It has 11 vowel letters, 39 consonant letters, and various diacritical marks. The Bengali script is notable for its rounded letter forms.

Telugu script is used for Telugu. It has approximately 56 letters and is closely related to Kannada script. Telugu script is known for its rounded, curving forms.

Kannada script is used for Kannada and closely related to Telugu script. It has approximately 49 primary letters plus additional symbols. Kannada has a rich tradition of inscriptions dating back to the 5th century CE.

Malayalam script is one of the most complex Indic scripts. It has 53 letters and hundreds of ligature characters. Malayalam text is visually distinctive due to its many curved and complex combined letter forms.

Gurmukhi script is used for Punjabi, particularly in the Indian Punjab. It was standardized by Guru Angad Dev in the 16th century. Gurmukhi has 35 consonants, 9 independent vowels, and various diacritics.
