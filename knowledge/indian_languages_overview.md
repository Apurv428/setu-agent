# Indian Languages Overview

India is home to hundreds of languages and dialects. The Constitution of India recognizes 22 official languages in the Eighth Schedule, known as the Scheduled Languages. These are: Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri (Meitei), Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu.

Hindi is the most widely spoken language in India, with approximately 600 million speakers. It serves as one of the two official languages of the central government, alongside English.

India has two dominant language families: the Indo-Aryan family, spoken in northern India, and the Dravidian family, spoken in southern India. Indo-Aryan languages include Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, and Punjabi. Dravidian languages include Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Malayalam.

The Linguistic Reorganisation of States in 1956 redrew the boundaries of Indian states largely along linguistic lines, reflecting the importance of language to Indian regional identity.

India's linguistic diversity is unparalleled. The 2001 census recorded 122 languages with more than 10,000 speakers each, and over 1,600 dialects. This diversity makes multilingual AI systems like Sarvam particularly important for reaching all of India's population.
